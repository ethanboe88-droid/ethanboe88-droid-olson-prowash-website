The short answer: **most homes in Northwest Wisconsin should be washed once a year**, with some
surfaces on a longer cycle and others on a shorter one. Our humidity, tree cover and long winters
push growth faster than in drier parts of the country, so the "every few years" advice you read
online runs a little optimistic here.

Below is a realistic schedule, followed by the signs that mean you should not wait for the calendar.

## Recommended cleaning frequency by surface

### House siding — every 12 months

An annual [house wash](/services#house-washing) keeps algae and mildew from getting a foothold,
protects the finish, and stops the green film that dulls your color. Homes under heavy tree cover or
near water often benefit from a lighter touch-up wash mid-season.

### Roof — every 2 to 3 years (soft wash only)

Black streaks on asphalt shingles are a cyanobacteria called *Gleocapsa magma*. A professional
[soft wash](/services#soft-washing) kills it at the root and buys you two to three years. Never
pressure wash a roof — it strips granules and voids many shingle warranties.

### Deck and patio — every 12 months

Wood and composite both hold mildew and turn gray-green under our humidity. An annual cleaning keeps
the surface safe underfoot and is essential prep if you plan to stain or seal.

### Driveway, walks and concrete — every 1 to 2 years

Flatwork collects leaf tannin, dirt, algae in shaded spots, and oil near the garage. Annual cleaning
keeps it bright; every other year is the minimum before staining sets in.

### Fences — every 2 years

### Gutters (exterior face) — with your annual house wash

## Signs you're already overdue

Don't wait for the anniversary if you see any of these:

- **Green or black patches** on siding, especially on the north and shaded sides
- **Dark streaks** running down the roof
- A **chalky residue** on your hand when you wipe the siding (oxidation trapping dirt)
- **Slippery spots** on the deck, steps or walkway — this is a slip hazard
- **Wasp nests, spider webs and mud daubers** collecting under eaves and soffits
- Concrete that has gone from gray to **green-gray or black** in shaded areas
- You're **listing the house** — a wash is one of the highest-ROI things you can do before photos

## Does frequent washing damage surfaces?

Not when it's done correctly. Damage comes from *too much pressure on the wrong surface*, not from
regular cleaning. The right method — soft washing for siding and roofs, controlled pressure for
hardscapes — is gentle enough to repeat every year for the life of the home. In fact, regular gentle
cleaning is easier on your exterior than letting growth dig in and then blasting it off.

## A low-effort maintenance plan

Most of our customers settle into a simple rhythm:

1. **Once a year:** house wash + gutter faces + driveway and walks, all in one visit.
2. **Every 2–3 years:** add the roof soft wash to that visit.
3. **As needed:** deck cleaning before you re-stain; a mid-summer touch-up if you're under heavy
   trees.
