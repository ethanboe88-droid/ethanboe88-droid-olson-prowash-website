The short answer: **most homes in the Lake Stevens area should be washed once a year**, with some
surfaces on a longer cycle and others on a shorter one. Our rain, marine humidity and evergreen
canopy push algae and moss faster than in drier parts of the country, so the "every few years"
advice you read online runs a little optimistic here.

Below is a realistic schedule, followed by the signs that mean you should not wait for the calendar.

## Recommended cleaning frequency by surface

### House siding — every 12 months

An annual [house wash](/services#house-washing) keeps algae and mildew from getting a foothold,
protects the finish, and stops the green film that dulls your color. Homes under heavy tree cover or
near the water often benefit from a lighter touch-up wash mid-year.

### Roof — every 1 to 2 years (soft wash only)

In the Pacific Northwest, roofs grow **moss and lichen**, not just black streaks. A professional
[soft wash](/services#soft-washing) kills it at the root and, paired with a zinc or copper treatment,
slows regrowth. Never pressure wash a roof — it strips granules and voids most shingle warranties.
Left alone, moss lifts shingle edges and traps water until the deck rots.

### Deck and patio — every 12 months

Wood and composite both grow a slick green film in our climate and stay damp for months. An annual
cleaning keeps the surface safe underfoot and is essential prep if you plan to stain or seal.

### Driveway, walks and concrete — every 12 months

Flatwork collects moss in the shade, needle and leaf tannin, dirt and oil near the garage. Once a
year keeps it bright; wait longer and the staining sets in.

### Fences — every 1 to 2 years

### Gutters (exterior face) — with your annual house wash

## Signs you're already overdue

Don't wait for the anniversary if you see any of these:

- **Green or black patches** on siding, especially on the north and shaded sides
- **Moss clumps or black streaks** on the roof, or moss in the gutters
- A **chalky residue** on your hand when you wipe the siding (oxidation trapping dirt)
- **Slippery spots** on the deck, steps or walkway — this is a slip hazard
- **Spider webs and mud daubers** collecting under eaves and soffits
- Concrete that has gone from gray to **green-gray or black** in shaded areas
- You're **listing the house** — a wash is one of the highest-ROI things you can do before photos

## Does frequent washing damage surfaces?

Not when it's done correctly. Damage comes from *too much pressure on the wrong surface*, not from
regular cleaning. The right method — soft washing for siding and roofs, controlled pressure for
hardscapes — is gentle enough to repeat every year for the life of the home. In fact, regular gentle
cleaning is easier on your exterior than letting moss and growth dig in and then blasting it off.

## A low-effort maintenance plan

Most of our customers settle into a simple rhythm:

1. **Once a year:** house wash + gutter faces + driveway and walks, all in one visit.
2. **Every 1–2 years:** add the roof soft wash and moss treatment to that visit.
3. **As needed:** deck cleaning before you re-stain; a mid-year touch-up if you're under heavy trees.
