The best windows to have your roof cleaned in Washington are **late spring (roughly April–June)**
and **early fall (September–October)**. Both give the treatment the conditions it works best in:
mild temperatures and a dry-enough stretch to dwell and rinse properly. You *can* clean a roof
most of the year here — but these are the sweet spots.

## Why late spring and early fall

**Temperatures are mild.** Roof cleaning solutions work by dwelling on the surface. In cold
weather they work slowly and can't be rinsed well; in high heat they dry out before they've done
the job. The 45–75°F range is ideal, and that's most of spring and fall in the
<a href="https://www.weather.gov/sew/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Seattle-area climate</a>.

**The roof isn't frozen or baking.** No frost on the shingles, no scorching midday surface temps.

**It lines up with the work you'd bundle.** Spring cleaning after the winter moss push, or fall
cleaning before the heavy rains return — either way you can fold in
[gutter cleaning](/services/gutter-cleaning) and a [house wash](/services/house-washing).

## Why not winter

- **Freezing temps.** Treatment can't dwell or rinse properly, and any water left on the roof can
  freeze. We won't treat in a hard freeze.
- **Short days and constant wet.** Hard to get a dry-enough window.
- **Safety.** Icy or frost-slick roofs and ladders are dangerous.

Light-growth roofs can still be done on a mild, dry winter day, but it's not the ideal window.

## Why not peak summer

Mid-July through August is our dry season, so access and rinsing are easy — that part's fine. The
catch is **moss goes dormant and turns brown** in the dry stretch, which makes it harder to see
exactly how far it's spread. It's also our busiest stretch, so booking is tighter.

If your roof clearly needs it, summer is perfectly workable — just know the moss is still there
under that brown, dormant look. See
[can rain wash moss off your roof](/blog/can-rain-wash-moss-off-roof) for why it greens right back
up in September.

## A simple seasonal guide

| Season | Roof cleaning? | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Spring (Apr–Jun)** | Best | Mild, post-winter moss push, easy to bundle |
| **Summer (Jul–Aug)** | Good | Easy access; moss looks dormant/brown; book early |
| **Early fall (Sep–Oct)** | Best | Mild, beats the heavy rains, great time to bundle gutters |
| **Late fall (Nov)** | OK | Watch the forecast; needs a dry, above-freezing window |
| **Winter (Dec–Feb)** | Limited | Light growth only, mild days, no freeze |

## What to watch for in your region

- **West of the Cascades / Puget Sound lowlands:** the schedule above. Shade and tree cover matter
  more than exact dates.
- **Foothills and higher elevations (Granite Falls, Gold Bar, Sultan):** shorter shoulder seasons,
  earlier frosts — aim for May–June and September.
- **Rain shadow areas (parts of the north Sound):** a bit more flexibility, but the same
  temperature rules apply.

## Frequently asked questions

**It's July and my roof looks brown, not green — is it fine?**
That's dormant moss, not dead moss. It'll green back up with the fall rain. Worth treating.

**Can you clean in the rain?**
A light drizzle during the dwell is sometimes fine; steady rain washes the treatment off before it
works. We schedule around the forecast.

**Should I wait until right before I sell?**
A pre-listing clean is high-return, but don't let the roof take damage waiting for that. Treat on
schedule regardless.

**How far ahead should I book?**
For spring and fall windows, two to four weeks. Summer fills up fastest.
