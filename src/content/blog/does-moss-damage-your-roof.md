Every so often someone tells me moss on a roof is "just cosmetic" — a Pacific Northwest look, like
green trim. I get why people want that to be true. It isn't. Moss is one of the few roof problems
that actively shortens the life of your shingles, and on a shaded Washington roof it does it fast.
Here's exactly how.

## How moss damages shingles

**It holds water against the roof.** A dry shingle sheds rain in seconds. A shingle with a moss
cushion on it stays damp for days. That constant moisture works into the shingle mat, degrades the
asphalt, and in freezing weather expands in the tiny gaps and pries things apart.

**It lifts shingle edges.** Moss doesn't just sit on top — it creeps *under* the bottom edge of
each shingle and grows there, jacking the shingle up. Once an edge is lifted, wind gets under it
and rain runs backward beneath it, straight onto the underlayment.

**Its rhizoids dig in.** Moss anchors with root-like rhizoids that work into the surface and
between granules. Pulling established moss off takes granules with it — which is why you kill it
first and remove it gently, never scrape a dry roof.

**It clogs the drainage path.** Moss and its debris wash into valleys and gutters, back water up
under the shingles, and keep the whole roof edge wetter than it should be.

The Asphalt Roofing Manufacturers Association (<a href="https://www.asphaltroofing.org/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">ARMA</a>) publishes
guidance specifically on roof algae and moss because of the damage and the warranty implications —
it's a recognized problem, not a myth.

## The timeline of ignoring it

| Stage | What's happening |
| --- | --- |
| **Year 1–2** | Green fuzz along edges and valleys, black algae streaking. Mostly cosmetic — but the roots are establishing. |
| **Year 3–5** | Thick moss cushions. Shingle edges lifting. Granule loss accelerating. Damp spots in the attic after long rains. |
| **Year 5+** | Underlayment saturated in spots, decking beginning to rot, leaks at valleys and penetrations. Now it's a repair, not a cleaning. |
| **The bill** | A $299–$950 [soft wash](/services/roof-cleaning) at year 2 vs. $1,500+ in repairs — or a $12,000–$25,000 full replacement years early. |

## Moss vs. algae vs. lichen — know what you're looking at

- **Algae** (*Gloeocapsa magma*): flat black streaks and stains running down the slopes. Doesn't
  hold much water but spreads fast and looks terrible. Feeds on the limestone filler in shingles.
- **Moss**: raised, green (brown when dormant), spongy cushions — worst in valleys, on north slopes
  and along shaded edges. This is the one that lifts shingles and holds moisture.
- **Lichen**: crusty, flat, gray-green patches that look almost painted on. Bonds tightly to the
  granule surface and is the most stubborn to remove — it needs a proper dwell time to release.

All three are handled by the same [soft wash](/services/soft-washing) approach: treat, let it
dwell to kill at the root, rinse, clear. Which is present just affects how long the treatment
needs to work.

## What to do about it

1. **Have it soft washed** — treated, killed at the root, rinsed, and the dead growth cleared.
   Never pressure washed; that strips granules and voids warranties.
2. **Keep debris off** — needles and leaves feed regrowth. Blow the roof off a couple times a
   year, or fold it into a [maintenance plan](/services/maintenance-plans).
3. **Trim overhanging branches** — more sun and airflow, less shade and drip.
4. **Clean the gutters** — a backed-up [gutter](/services/gutter-cleaning) keeps the whole roof
   edge wet.

## Frequently asked questions

**If I kill the moss, do I still have to remove it?**
Yes — dead moss still holds water and looks bad. We clear it as part of the service; heavy mats
sometimes release over a couple weeks and need a quick return pass.

**Can moss be prevented completely?**
Not in this climate. You manage it — treat on a 2–3 year cycle and keep the roof clear between
times.

**Is a little algae streaking worth treating?**
It's the cheapest time to treat. Streaking spreads every wet season and the stain sets in.

**Will my homeowners insurance cover moss damage?**
Generally no — insurers treat it as deferred maintenance, and a moss-covered roof can even
jeopardize a claim or renewal.
