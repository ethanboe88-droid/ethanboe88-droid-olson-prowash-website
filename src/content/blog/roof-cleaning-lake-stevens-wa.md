I grew up around here, and I've cleaned a lot of roofs between the lake, Frontier Village and the
hills out toward Machias. Lake Stevens has its own particular roof problems, and knowing them is
half of doing the job right. Here's what to expect from a proper roof cleaning locally, and how to
pick a company that won't cost you a roof.

## Why Lake Stevens roofs struggle

**The lake and the trees.** Homes near the water sit in humid air that lingers. Homes up in the
timber get shade most of the day and a steady rain of fir and cedar needles. Both hold moisture on
the shingles, and moisture is what moss, algae and lichen need.

**North slopes.** Any roof face pointing north or northeast barely sees sun from October to April.
That's where you'll see the first green fuzz and the black streaking.

**Older subdivisions.** A lot of Lake Stevens housing stock is 20–35 years old, which means a lot
of roofs are on their second shingle layer or nearing the end of the first. Moss on an aging roof
is a bigger deal — there's less shingle life left to protect.

**Valleys and low-slope sections.** Additions, porch roofs and dormer valleys collect needle
litter, stay wet, and grow the thickest moss on the house.

## What a proper roof cleaning looks like

A correct job on an asphalt shingle roof is a **soft wash**, not a pressure wash:

1. **Inspect.** We walk the roof and look in the gutters before quoting — pitch, shingle
   condition, moss severity, problem valleys.
2. **Protect.** Plants, downspout outlets and landscaping get pre-wet and covered.
3. **Treat.** Our in-house roof treatment goes on at low pressure and is left to **dwell**, which
   kills moss, algae and lichen at the root.
4. **Rinse.** A full, careful rinse once the treatment has worked.
5. **Clear.** Loosened moss and debris off the roof and out of the gutters.

It's done to <a href="https://www.asphaltroofing.org/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">ARMA</a> and shingle-manufacturer cleaning guidelines
so your warranty stays intact. See our full
[roof cleaning service page](/services/roof-cleaning) for the details, and our
[soft wash vs pressure wash guide](/blog/soft-wash-vs-pressure-wash-roof) for why the method
matters.

## How to choose a roof cleaning company

**Confirm licensed and insured.** Washington makes this easy — look the company up on the
<a href="https://www.lni.wa.gov/licensing-permits/contractors/verify-a-contractor-tradesperson-or-business/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">L&I contractor verification</a>
site. We're registered as #OLSONLP747MF, bonded and insured. If a roof cleaner isn't insured and
someone gets hurt on your property, that's your problem.

**Ask what pressure they use on shingles.** The right answer is "low pressure, the solution does
the work." If they talk about blasting or a certain PSI on the shingles, keep calling.

**Look for local reviews and real photos.** Ours are on our [reviews page](/reviews) and
[gallery](/gallery). Generic stock photos and no named local reviews are a warning sign.

**Get the scope in writing.** Roof, gutters cleared, plants protected, rinse included — it should
all be spelled out.

## Red flags to avoid

- A whole-roof price under $299 — corners are being cut
- "Pressure washing" your shingles
- Door-knockers who need a decision today and cash up front
- No written scope, no license number, no insurance certificate
- Promises that moss will "never come back" — impossible here; be skeptical
