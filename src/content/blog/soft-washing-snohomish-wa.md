Snohomish is one of our closer stops — about ten minutes from our Lake Stevens shop — and it's a
town of two very different exterior-cleaning problems living a few blocks apart. Downtown around
First Street, the houses are a century old. Out along the Pilchuck and Snohomish river valley,
the problem isn't age, it's moisture. Both call for the same answer: a low-pressure
[soft wash](/services/soft-washing/), never a pressure washer.

## Why historic Snohomish needs soft washing, not pressure

The First Street Historic District is full of original Victorian and Craftsman homes with old wood
lap siding, hand-glazed trim and window details that were never built to take a pressure washer.
High pressure drives water behind aging siding, strips soft old paint down to bare wood, and can
etch or crack trim that's a hundred years old. The National Park Service's guidance on caring for
historic buildings is blunt about it: gentle, low-pressure methods are the standard for anything
of this age, and aggressive cleaning is one of the more common ways an older building gets
damaged. See the
<a href="https://www.nps.gov/tps/how-to-preserve/briefs.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">National Park Service's Preservation Briefs</a>
if you want the technical version.

A [soft wash](/services/soft-washing/) does the opposite — a cleaning solution does the work at
garden-hose pressure, so a 100-year-old sill or a hand-painted porch rail comes clean without any
force against it.

## The river valley has its own problem: shade and moisture

Move a few minutes out of downtown toward Pilchuck, Cathcart, Three Lakes or Clearview and the
issue flips from "old and delicate" to "damp and shaded." River fog off the Snohomish and
Pilchuck rivers sits in the valley for hours on cool mornings, and heavy tree cover keeps roofs,
decks and north-facing walls wet well after the rest of the county has dried out. Washington State
University's
<a href="https://hortsense.cahnr.wsu.edu/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Hortsense</a>
resource is clear that shade plus standing moisture is exactly what grows moss and algae fastest —
which is why river-valley homes here often need attention a little more often than town.

## How we handle an older home

1. **Walk it first.** We look at the siding, trim and any failing paint before quoting, so we know
   what the surface can take.
2. **Pre-wet and protect.** Landscaping, window seals and anything already loose gets covered or
   handled with extra care.
3. **Low-pressure apply.** The cleaning solution goes on at garden-hose pressure — no force against
   old wood or glazing.
4. **Dwell, then rinse.** The solution does the work; the rinse just carries it away.

## Frequently asked questions

**Will soft washing hurt my old paint or wood siding?**
No — that's the whole reason to use it instead of a pressure washer. Low pressure and the right
dilution lift dirt, algae and mildew without stripping paint or driving water behind the boards.
We'll flag anything already failing before we start.

**My house is on the historic register — does that change anything?**
It changes how carefully we work, not what we do. Soft washing is the method preservation
guidance recommends for exactly this reason. Tell us if there are specific finishes or details you
want extra care around.

**Do river-valley homes need cleaning more often than downtown?**
Often yes. Heavy shade and river fog keep north walls and roofs damp longer, so moss and algae
come back faster. See
[how often should you clean your roof in the Pacific Northwest](/blog/how-often-clean-roof-pacific-northwest/)
for a general schedule.

**Can you do the roof and deck the same visit as the house wash?**
Yes — it's our most common Snohomish booking and costs less than three separate trips.

**Do you serve the areas just outside town?**
Yes — Pilchuck, Cathcart, Three Lakes, Clearview, Machias and the rest of our
[service area](/service-area/).
