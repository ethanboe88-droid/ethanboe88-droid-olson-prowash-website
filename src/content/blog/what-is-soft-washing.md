"Soft washing" gets used as a buzzword, so here's the plain version: **soft washing is cleaning a
surface with a detergent solution and low pressure instead of high pressure.** The chemistry does
the work; the water just rinses. It's the standard method for anything on a house that a pressure
washer would damage — which is most of the house.

## How soft washing works

1. **Apply a cleaning solution.** A blend of detergents, surfactants and a cleaning agent (usually
   a controlled dilution of sodium hypochlorite for biological growth) is applied at low pressure.
2. **Let it dwell.** The solution sits for several minutes and breaks down the organic
   growth — algae, moss spores, mildew, lichen — at the root, plus the general grime and film.
3. **Agitate if needed.** Heavier areas get a soft brush.
4. **Rinse gently.** A low-pressure rinse carries everything away. The pressure at the surface is
   about what comes out of a garden hose.

The key difference from pressure washing: nothing is being *blasted*. A pressure washer at
2,000–4,000 PSI removes dirt by force. Soft washing removes it by killing and dissolving it, so it
works on delicate surfaces and it kills growth at the root instead of just knocking off the top
layer — which is why the results last two to three times longer.

## The chemistry, briefly

- **Surfactants** break the surface tension of water so the solution spreads into pores and film
  instead of beading off. This is what lets a low-pressure application actually clean.
- **Sodium hypochlorite**, properly diluted, kills algae, mold, mildew and moss. It's the same
  active ingredient as household disinfectants. The EPA maintains
  <a href="https://www.epa.gov/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">registration and safety information</a> on hypochlorite products and their
  proper use.
- **A rinse agent / neutralizer** helps the solution clear cleanly and protects landscaping.

Concentrations matter enormously — this is where experience counts. Too weak and it doesn't work;
too strong and you can lighten a stain or stress a plant. A good soft wash is dialed to the surface
and the growth.

## What soft washing is best for

| Surface | Method |
| --- | --- |
| Asphalt shingle & metal [roofs](/services/roof-cleaning) | Soft wash — always |
| Vinyl, fiber-cement, aluminum, wood [siding](/services/house-washing) | Soft wash |
| Stucco and EIFS | Soft wash |
| Painted or stained wood, trim, soffits, fascia | Soft wash |
| Composite decking, pergolas, fences | Soft wash / low pressure |
| Concrete driveways, walks, [patios](/services/driveway-concrete-cleaning) | Pressure wash (surface cleaner) |
| Pavers, stone, unpainted masonry | Pressure wash |

The rule of thumb: if it's porous and tough, pressure wash it. If it's coated, layered, or up
high, soft wash it. Our

surface by surface.

## Is it safe for plants and pets?

Yes, when it's done properly. We pre-wet all landscaping so the solution can't concentrate on
foliage, tarp sensitive plants, keep the mix at the right strength, and rinse everything
thoroughly afterward. Runoff is controlled per
<a href="https://ecology.wa.gov/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Washington Department of Ecology</a> guidance. Pets should stay inside
during the wash and off treated surfaces until dry — same as after mopping with any cleaner.

## Why it's better for your home

- **No granule loss** on roofs, no forced water under siding, no etched wood
- **Kills growth at the root** — 2–3 year results instead of one season
- **Even, streak-free finish** — no "zebra stripes" from an inconsistent pressure pass
- **Warranty-safe** — it's the method <a href="https://www.asphaltroofing.org/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">ARMA</a> and shingle
  manufacturers specify
- **Gets into texture** — stucco, wood grain and lap siding seams clean better with solution than
  with a pressure cone

## Frequently asked questions

**Does soft washing use bleach?**
It uses a controlled, diluted form of sodium hypochlorite for biological growth, thoroughly rinsed
and buffered. It's not the same as pouring laundry bleach on your house — concentration and rinsing
are the whole game.

**Will it damage my landscaping?**
Not with proper pre-wetting and rinsing. We've soft washed thousands of square feet next to prize
gardens without issue.

**How long does a soft wash last?**
Roofs 2–3 years; house siding often looks clean for 2+ years depending on shade and exposure.

**Can I soft wash my own house?**
The mixing and the ladder work are the risky parts. Getting the concentration wrong either wastes
your time or lightens your siding, and most homeowners don't have a safe way to reach a second
story.

**Does it smell?**
There's a faint chlorine smell during the wash that clears as it dries and rinses.
