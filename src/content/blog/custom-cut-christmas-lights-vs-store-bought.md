Every roofline in the neighborhood has the same lights on it in December, but a few houses look
noticeably sharper — even, tight lines that trace the architecture with no dark spots. That look
almost never comes from a box of strings off the shelf. It comes from **custom-cut lights**, and
the gap between the two approaches shows from the street.

## The store-string problem

Retail light sets come in fixed lengths — usually 25 feet. Your roofline doesn't. So every run
ends up with one of two compromises:

- **Bunching at the corners.** The leftover footage has to go somewhere, so it piles up where two
  runs meet and reads as a bright knot.
- **Dark gaps.** Cut the set short to avoid the bunch and you leave the last few feet of the peak
  unlit.

Then there's the slack looped back along the gutter, the male/female connectors stacking up three
deep at each junction, and the failure rate: **cheap strings are wired in series**, so one dead
bulb can take out a whole section, and by New Year's a good fraction of the display is dark.

## What "custom-cut" means

We measure every **roofline, ridge, peak, gable and window run** on the house, then **cut and
end-cap each strand to the exact length** of that section. No leftover to hide, no gap to live
with. The bulbs are spaced evenly the whole way, the line sits tight to the edge, and the wiring
is routed out of sight and tied into a timer.

The bulbs and wire are **commercial-grade** — C9, C7 or mini, on weather-rated wire built for
Northwest wind, rain and freeze-thaw, not the thin PVC that goes brittle by January. Individual
bulbs are replaceable without losing the run.
