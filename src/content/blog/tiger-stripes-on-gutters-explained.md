You had the gutters cleaned, the downspouts flow fine, and the roof looks great — but the gutters
still look dirty from the street. Long black vertical streaks run down the front face of every
run, worst under the valleys and roof edges. That's **tiger striping**, and it's the single most
common thing homeowners around Lake Stevens ask us to fix after a house wash.

## What tiger stripes actually are

They are not loose dirt, and they are not mold. Every time it rains, water sheets off the roof
carrying a fine mix of **asphalt-shingle residue, roof grit, pollen and airborne pollutant
fallout**. That slurry runs over the lip of the gutter and down the outside face. The water
evaporates; the residue stays. Over months and years it **oxidizes and bonds to the painted
finish** in a striped pattern that follows the drip lines — hence "tiger stripes."

Because it's a bonded film rather than surface grime, the usual tools do nothing:

- **A garden hose or pressure washer** just sheets water over it. You can stand there for ten
  minutes and watch the stripes not move.
- **A regular [gutter cleaning](/services/gutter-cleaning)** clears the *inside* of the trough —
  leaves, needle sludge, downspout clogs. It was never meant to touch the outside face, so a
  freshly cleaned gutter can still be covered in stripes.
- **Scrubbing by hand** with dish soap lightens them slightly and takes forever, and you'll
  usually leave swirl marks or miss the high sections you can't reach safely.

## Why they come back if you ignore them

The stripes are a symptom of runoff, so they keep forming as long as the roof sheds water — which
is always. Homes that stripe fastest have **older asphalt roofs** shedding more granule, sit
**under heavy tree cover**, or are on **busier roads** where there's more road film in the air.
The longer the oxidation sits, the harder it bonds, so a set of stripes that's one season old
comes off far easier than one that's been baking on for five years.

## How gutter brightening removes them

[Gutter brightening](/services/gutter-brightening) is a dedicated detergent process, not pressure
washing. On our jobs it runs like this:

1. **Protect below.** Siding, plants and walkways under the run get wetted down and covered so the
   cleaner can't dry on anything it shouldn't.
2. **Apply the brightener.** A gutter-specific detergent goes onto the faces and end caps and is
   left to **dwell** — it needs a few minutes to break the bond between the oxidized film and the
   paint.
3. **Agitate the heavy sections.** The worst stripes under the valleys get worked by hand with a
   soft brush while the detergent is active.
4. **Rinse and check.** The whole line is rinsed at **low pressure** — enough to carry the
   dissolved film away, gentle enough to leave the factory finish intact — then we walk it and
   re-treat anything that held on.

Most single-family homes take an hour or two depending on linear footage and how set-in the
staining is.

## The honest limit

If your gutters are **very old and chalky**, the paint itself may already be oxidized and
sun-faded under the stripes. Brightening will pull the black streaking off, but the finish
underneath can still look dull or uneven — it can't restore paint that's gone. We'll tell you
that after a look rather than promising showroom-white on a 20-year-old gutter.
