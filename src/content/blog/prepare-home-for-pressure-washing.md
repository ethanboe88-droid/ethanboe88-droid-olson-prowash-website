Prepping for a wash takes about fifteen minutes and makes the whole day go smoother — faster work,
a better result, and nothing of yours in the splash zone. Here's the checklist we send customers
before a [house wash](/services/house-washing) or [pressure washing](/services/pressure-washing)
visit.

## The homeowner checklist

**Close all windows and doors — tightly.**
Check the ones you never open, especially older single-hung windows and basement windows. Latch
them. If a screen is loose or torn, take it out.

**Move vehicles.**
Pull cars, the boat and the trailer out of the driveway and away from the work area — a car length
of clearance from the house is plenty. Overspray with detergent is easy to rinse off immediately
but annoying if it dries.

**Clear the perimeter.**
Move patio furniture, grills, planters, door mats, garden tools, kids' toys, hoses and décor away
from the walls and off the driveway. We'll move lightweight stuff if needed, but it's faster if
it's already clear.

**Point out your plants.**
We pre-wet and protect landscaping as standard, but tell us about anything you're precious
about — a new transplant, a vegetable bed, a specimen shrub — and we'll give it extra cover and
rinsing.

**Secure pets.**
Keep dogs and cats inside during the wash and off any treated surface until it's fully dry. Pick up
the yard beforehand so nothing gets in the way.

**Cap or cover exterior outlets and fixtures.**
Modern exterior outlets are weather-rated, but for older homes it doesn't hurt to shut off power to
exterior outlets at the breaker and tape covers on. Point out any low-voltage landscape lighting,
security cameras or a doorbell camera.

**Note problem spots.**
Wasp nests, a soft piece of trim, a window that leaks, a section of failing paint, a dryer vent
that's come loose — a two-minute heads-up saves trouble.

**Provide water access.**
Make sure the outdoor spigot is on and working. If it's winterized or low-pressure, let us know so
we can plan.

**Move indoor items away from washed walls.**
On interior wall cavities that are known to leak (rare, but it happens on old houses), move framed
photos or electronics on the other side of that wall as a precaution.

## What the crew handles

- Pre-wetting and covering landscaping
- Moving lightweight furniture and putting it back
- Protecting downspout outlets and light fixtures as needed
- Choosing the right method and pressure for each surface — [soft wash](/services/soft-washing)
  for siding and painted surfaces, controlled pressure for concrete
- Containing and directing runoff away from storm drains, per
  <a href="https://ecology.wa.gov/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Washington Department of Ecology</a> guidance
- A full rinse and a walkthrough with you at the end

## What NOT to do

- **Don't leave windows cracked "for air."** One open window is a wet windowsill.
- **Don't pressure wash the areas yourself first.** It doesn't save time and it can drive water
  into places that then take days to dry.
- **Don't run sprinklers** the morning of — wet, muddy beds make plant protection harder.
- **Don't stand in the work zone.** The <a href="https://www.cpsc.gov/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">U.S. Consumer Product Safety Commission</a>
  logs thousands of pressure-washer injuries a year; the spray will cut skin. Watch from a window.
- **Don't repaint or seal right before.** Let us clean first — fresh paint or sealer can be
  damaged, and clean, dry substrate is what you want under a new coat anyway.

## Frequently asked questions

**Do I need to be home?**
No, as long as we have water access and know the gate code and where the pets are. Most customers
leave for the day. We'll text before and after.

**How long does it take?**
A driveway is under two hours. A full house wash is two to four hours depending on size and
stories.

**Will water get inside?**
Not with windows properly closed and the right method for each surface. Soft washing siding uses
garden-hose pressure — it's gentler than a rainstorm.

**What about my garden right against the house?**
Tell us where it is. Pre-wet, covered, rinsed — we do this on every job.

**Can you do the roof and gutters the same day?**
Yes — [roof cleaning](/services/roof-cleaning) and [gutter cleaning](/services/gutter-cleaning)
bundle well with a house wash and cost less together.
