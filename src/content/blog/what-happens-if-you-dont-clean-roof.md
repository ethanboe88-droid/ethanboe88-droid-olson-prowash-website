Skipping roof cleaning in western Washington isn't a neutral choice — it's a slow, compounding one.
Here's what actually happens, year by year, and what it costs compared with staying ahead of it.

## The damage timeline

**Year 1–2 — cosmetic (but the clock's started).**
Green fuzz along the shingle edges and in the valleys. Black algae streaks down the slopes. Looks
bad from the street; the roof is still structurally fine. This is the cheapest possible time to
treat it — a straightforward [soft wash](/services/roof-cleaning), $299–$950 for most homes.

**Year 3–5 — structural.**
Moss cushions thicken and creep *under* the shingle edges, lifting them. Wind gets under lifted
shingles; rain runs backward beneath them. Granule loss accelerates — you'll see grit in the
gutters. Lichen bonds to the surface. After a long rain you might notice a musty smell or a stain
on the attic sheathing. Now you're looking at a heavier cleaning plus possible shingle repairs.

**Year 5+ — expensive.**
Underlayment saturated in spots. Decking starting to rot at valleys and penetrations. Active leaks.
A moss-jacked shingle field that's lost too many granules to recover. At this point a cleaning
can't save it — you're replacing the roof, often 5–10 years earlier than you should have needed
to.

| | Stay ahead of it | Let it go |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cost over 10 years | ~3–4 soft washes at $299–$950 each | $12,000–$25,000 replacement, years early |
| Roof lifespan | Full manufacturer life | Cut short by 5–10 years |
| Attic / interior | Dry | Stains, mustiness, possible mold remediation |

The Asphalt Roofing Manufacturers Association (<a href="https://www.asphaltroofing.org/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">ARMA</a>) publishes
guidance on algae and moss precisely because letting it go is a warranty and longevity issue, not
a cosmetic preference.

## Insurance implications

Homeowners insurance covers **sudden, accidental** damage — a windstorm, a falling branch. It does
**not** cover **wear, rot and neglect.** A moss-covered roof that finally leaks is almost always
denied as deferred maintenance, per how insurers describe roof claims (see the
<a href="https://www.iii.org/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Insurance Information Institute</a>).

It gets worse: during a routine inspection or at renewal, a visibly moss-covered or aged roof can
get you **non-renewed** or forced onto a costly actual-cash-value roof endorsement. Insurers in
the Pacific Northwest have gotten strict about roof condition. A clean, maintained roof is part of
keeping your policy affordable.

## Curb appeal and home value

A green, streaked roof is the first thing a buyer's eye lands on, and it reads as "deferred
maintenance — what else did they skip?" Real-estate agents routinely have roofs cleaned before
listing because it's one of the highest-return prep items. Appraisers and inspectors note roof
condition directly. A [house wash](/services/house-washing) and roof cleaning together are a
few hundred dollars that can move a sale price by thousands and shorten days-on-market.

## What "staying ahead of it" looks like

1. **Soft wash every 2–3 years** — treated, killed at the root, rinsed, cleared. Never pressure
   washed.
2. **Keep debris off** the roof and out of the [gutters](/services/gutter-cleaning) — needles and
   leaves feed regrowth and keep the edge wet.
3. **Trim overhanging branches** for sun and airflow.
4. **Consider a [maintenance plan](/services/maintenance-plans)** if your roof is heavily shaded.

## Frequently asked questions

**My roof is 15 years old — is it even worth cleaning?**
Often yes, if the shingles are still pliable and granule loss isn't severe. We'll give you an
honest read — sometimes the answer is "spend the money toward replacement instead," and we'll tell
you that.

**How bad is too bad?**
If shingles are brittle, cracking, curling hard, or you have active leaks, cleaning won't reverse
it. A soft wash is prevention and maintenance, not repair.

**Can I just do the streaks and skip the moss?**
The moss is the part that damages the roof. If anything, skip the cosmetic streaking and deal with
the moss.

**Will cleaning void anything?**
No — a proper low-pressure soft wash is compliant with shingle warranties. Pressure washing is what
voids them.
