Short, honest answer: **no.** Rain does not wash moss off your roof. If anything, rain is what
grows it. I hear this hope a lot — "maybe a good storm will knock it loose" — and I understand why,
but it's backwards.

## Why rain makes moss worse, not better

Moss is a plant. It has no true roots, so it pulls all its water straight out of the air and off
the surface it's sitting on. Constant moisture is exactly what it wants. Every rainy week in
western Washington is a growth spurt.

Washington State University's <a href="https://hortsense.cahnr.wsu.edu/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Hortsense</a> describes moss as an
indicator of shade and moisture — the wetter and shadier the spot, the more moss. A roof that
stays damp for months because of our climate is a moss farm, and rain is the irrigation.

Rain also doesn't have the force to remove anything. The <a href="https://www.weather.gov/sew/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">National Weather Service</a>
would call our typical rainfall "light to moderate" — it falls straight down at a few miles an
hour. Moss anchors into the shingle surface with rhizoids and grips between the granules. A
vertical drizzle isn't moving it. A pressure washer at 3,000 PSI would move it — and take your
shingle granules with it, which is why you never do that either.

## The myths, and the reality

**Myth: "A hard rainstorm will clear it."**
Reality: The storm waters it. Dead-of-winter is when moss looks its worst *because* it's the
wettest.

**Myth: "It goes away on its own in summer."**
Reality: It goes *dormant* and turns brown in the dry stretch, then greens back up with the first
September rain. It never left. See
[how long does roof cleaning last](/blog/how-long-does-roof-cleaning-last).

**Myth: "Moss is just cosmetic, so who cares if it stays."**
Reality: Moss lifts shingle edges and holds water against the roof, shortening its life. Full
breakdown in [does moss actually damage your roof](/blog/does-moss-damage-your-roof).

**Myth: "I'll just scrape it off with a broom."**
Reality: Dry scraping tears granules off the shingles and leaves the root structure and spores
behind, so it regrows in a couple months — now on a damaged roof.

## What actually clears a roof

A [soft wash](/services/soft-washing): a cleaning solution applied at low pressure, left to
**dwell** so it kills the moss, algae and lichen at the root, then a gentle rinse and a clearing
of the dead growth. No force, no granule loss, warranty-safe per
<a href="https://www.asphaltroofing.org/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">ARMA</a> guidelines. That's the whole trick — you kill it, you
don't blast it.

After that, keeping the roof clear of fir needles and leaf litter and trimming back overhanging
branches is what keeps rain from growing it back quickly. Our
[roof cleaning service](/services/roof-cleaning) covers the treatment; a
[maintenance plan](/services/maintenance-plans) covers keeping it clear.

## Frequently asked questions

**So there's genuinely nothing rain does to help?**
Correct. It's neutral at best and fuel at worst.

**What about a roof that faces south and gets sun?**
Sunny, open roofs dry out between rains and grow far less moss. It's the shaded and north-facing
roofs that stay wet and green.

**If I do nothing, does it plateau?**
No — it spreads to more of the roof each wet season and thickens where it already is.

**Can I put something on the roof so rain keeps it clean?**
Not really. A proper treatment now plus keeping debris off is what works. We don't install zinc
strips — they streak the roof and fade out.
