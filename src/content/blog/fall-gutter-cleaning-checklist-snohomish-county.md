In Snohomish County, gutters go from "fine" to "overflowing" in about three weeks every October.
The big-leaf maples drop all at once, the firs and cedars shed needles right behind them, and then
the real rain starts. Getting the gutters cleared *before* that sequence is one of the highest-
value hour of maintenance you'll do all year.

## Why fall gutter cleaning matters here

Western Washington gets the bulk of its 35–60+ inches of annual rain from mid-fall through spring.
A clogged gutter during that stretch doesn't just overflow once — it overflows every rain for
months. The <a href="https://www.energy.gov/energysaver/energy-saver" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">U.S. Department of Energy</a> and most
building-science guidance put roof and foundation water management near the top of the list for
preventing expensive damage, and functioning gutters are the first link in that chain.

## What clogged gutters actually cause

- **Foundation problems.** Water sheeting off the roofline pools against the foundation, saturates
  the soil, and over time causes settling, cracks and crawlspace or basement flooding.
- **Fascia and soffit rot.** Water backing up behind the gutter sits against the fascia board.
  Wet wood rots, paint fails, and eventually the gutter pulls loose.
- **Roof-edge damage.** Backed-up water wicks under the shingle edge and rots the decking at the
  eave — the most expensive part of the roof to repair.
- **Ice and heavy-load damage.** A gutter full of wet debris is heavy; add a cold snap and it can
  bend hangers or tear away.
- **Landscape washout and siding splash-back.** Overflow craters the beds below and throws mud and
  organic staining up onto the siding.

## DIY fall gutter cleaning checklist

**Before you start:** a stable ladder on firm ground, someone home with you, work gloves, safety
glasses. If it's a two-story or the roof is steep, stop here and hire it out — see the
<a href="https://www.cpsc.gov/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">U.S. Consumer Product Safety Commission</a> on ladder-fall injuries, which
spike every fall.

1. **Scoop the troughs.** Work from the ladder, not the roof. Bag the debris; don't drop it in the
   beds.
2. **Check every downspout.** Flush from the top with a hose. If water backs up, the elbow or the
   vertical run is clogged — clear it from the bottom or tap it loose.
3. **Flow-test the whole system.** Run water at the far end of each run and watch it reach the
   downspout. Standing water means a low spot or a pitch problem.
4. **Check the pitch and hangers.** Gutters should fall about ¼ inch per 10 feet toward the
   downspout. Re-secure loose hangers and spikes.
5. **Look at the seams and end caps.** Reseal anything dripping.
6. **Inspect the fascia behind the gutter.** Soft, dark or peeling wood means water's been sitting
   there — address it before winter.
7. **Clear the roof valleys** while you're up there — needle piles in the valleys feed the next
   clog.
8. **Point the downspout extensions away** from the foundation — at least 4–6 feet out.

## When to call a pro

- Two-story or steep-roof homes
- Gutters that clog again within weeks (that's a guard or a bigger-picture problem)
- Any sign of fascia rot, pull-away, or chronic overflow
- You'd just rather not spend a Saturday on a ladder in the cold

## Frequently asked questions

**How often do gutters need cleaning in Snohomish County?**
Twice a year minimum — late spring and mid-to-late fall. Heavy tree cover means three or four.

**When exactly in fall?**
After the bulk of the leaf drop, usually late October to mid-November, but before the sustained
rain sets in. Sometimes that means a second quick pass in December.
