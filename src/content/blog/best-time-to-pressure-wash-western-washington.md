In Western Washington the calendar matters more than it does almost anywhere else. Nine months of
moisture means moss and algae get a long growing season — and it means timing your exterior
cleaning well is the difference between a result that lasts a year and one that lasts a season.

## The sweet spot: late spring through early fall

**April through September** is the ideal window for most exterior cleaning in the Puget Sound
region:

- **Drier, warmer weather** helps detergents work and surfaces dry properly.
- **The big pollen and seed drop is over** by late spring, so a [house wash](/services/house-washing)
  in May or June stays clean through summer.
- **You get the whole dry season** to enjoy the result before the rain returns.
- **Longer days** mean we can schedule bigger jobs — combined roof, house and gutter visits — in
  one trip.

If you only clean your home's exterior once a year, **late spring is the time to do it.**

## Timing each job so it lasts

### Roof cleaning — spring or early fall

A [roof soft wash](/services/roof-cleaning) in spring clears the moss that thickened over winter
before it can damage anything. Early fall works too, and has a bonus: it clears the roof right
before the heavy leaf and needle drop, so your freshly cleaned [gutters](/services/gutter-cleaning)
do not immediately fill with dead moss.

### Gutter cleaning — twice, on the shoulders of the wet season

**Late fall**, after the leaves and needles have come down, is the critical one — clogged gutters
in a Pacific Northwest winter send water into the fascia and foundation. **Late spring** clears the
seed and blossom debris and the maple "helicopters." Homes with lots of firs nearby genuinely need
both.

### House washing — late spring

Wash after the pollen and cottonwood fluff have finished, and the [green algae film](/services/house-washing)
on the north and east walls comes off with it. You then get the full dry season looking clean.

### Deck cleaning — early summer, if you are sealing

Clean and brighten in late spring or early summer so the wood has a reliable dry stretch to accept
stain or sealer. Cleaning alone can happen any time.

## Do not wait for "perfect" weather

The most expensive approach is to keep putting it off. Moss that gets another winter to establish
is harder — and more expensive — to remove, and a gutter that overflows through a wet January can
cause damage that costs far more than the cleaning would have.
