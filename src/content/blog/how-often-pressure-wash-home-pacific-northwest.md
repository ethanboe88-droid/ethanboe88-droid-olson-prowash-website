Short answer: **most homes around Lake Stevens and the Puget Sound should be washed once a year**,
with roofs on a slightly shorter cycle than the "every few years" advice you read online. Our
rain, marine humidity and evergreen canopy push moss and algae faster than almost anywhere else in
the country.

Here's a realistic schedule, followed by the signs that mean you shouldn't wait for the calendar.

## Recommended frequency by surface

### House siding — every 12 months

An annual [house wash](/services#house-washing) keeps algae and mildew from getting a foothold,
protects the finish, and stops the green film that dulls your color. Homes under heavy tree cover
or near the water often benefit from a lighter touch-up wash mid-year.

### Roof — every 1 to 2 years (soft wash only)

In the PNW, roofs grow **moss and lichen**, not just black streaks — and moss is the bigger threat.
A professional [roof soft wash](/services#roof-cleaning) kills it at the root, and a zinc or copper
treatment slows regrowth. Never pressure wash a roof: it strips granules and voids most shingle
warranties. Left alone, moss lifts shingle edges and traps water until the deck rots.

### Deck and patio — every 12 months

Wood and composite both grow a slick green film here and stay damp for months. An annual cleaning
keeps the surface safe underfoot and is essential prep if you plan to stain or seal.

### Driveway, walks and concrete — every 12 months

Flatwork collects moss in the shade, needle and leaf tannin, dirt and oil near the garage. Once a
year keeps it bright; wait longer and the staining sets in.

### Gutters — twice a year

With this much conifer debris, spring and fall clean-outs are the realistic minimum. Bundle the
exterior [gutter face wash](/services#gutter-cleaning) with your annual house wash.

### Fences — every 1 to 2 years

## Signs you're already overdue

- **Moss clumps or black streaks** on the roof, or moss growing in the gutters
- **Green or black patches** on siding, especially on the north and shaded sides
- A **chalky residue** on your hand when you wipe the siding
- **Slippery spots** on the deck, steps or walkway — a genuine slip hazard
- Concrete gone from gray to **green-gray or black** in shaded areas
- You're **listing the house** — a wash is one of the highest-ROI things you can do before photos

## Does frequent washing damage surfaces?

Not when it's done correctly. Damage comes from *too much pressure on the wrong surface*, not from
regular cleaning. The right method — soft washing for roofs and siding, controlled pressure for
hardscapes — is gentle enough to repeat every year for the life of the home. Regular gentle
cleaning is far easier on your exterior than letting moss dig in and then blasting it off.

## A low-effort maintenance plan

1. **Once a year:** house wash + gutter faces + driveway and walks, in one visit.
2. **Every 1–2 years:** add the roof soft wash and moss treatment to that visit.
3. **Twice a year:** gutter clean-outs (spring and fall).
4. **As needed:** deck cleaning before you re-stain.
