Moss is the Pacific Northwest's signature yard problem. It grows on roofs, north-facing walls,
brick walkways, patio pavers, retaining walls and fences — anywhere that stays damp and shaded,
which west of the Cascades is nearly everywhere from October to May. Here's what it costs to have
it professionally removed in 2026, and what you're actually paying for.

## Why Washington grows so much moss

Two reasons: **rain and shade.** Western Washington gets 35–60+ inches of rain a year, most of it
in a long, cool, cloudy stretch when surfaces never fully dry. Add an evergreen canopy that blocks
what little winter sun there is, and you get the exact conditions moss needs — constant moisture,
low light, and cool temperatures. Washington State University's
<a href="https://hortsense.cahnr.wsu.edu/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Hortsense</a> resource describes moss as an indicator of those
site conditions rather than a disease you can cure once. That matters for how you budget: moss
removal is maintenance, not a one-time fix.

## "Removal only" vs. treatment — what's the difference?

Some outfits offer a cheap "scrape and blow" — physically knocking the moss off and leaving. It
looks fine for a month, then it's back, because the root structure and spores are still there. On a
roof, aggressive scraping also tears up shingle granules.

What we do instead: apply a cleaning treatment, let it **dwell** so it kills the moss, algae and
lichen at the root, then gently remove the dead growth and rinse. On concrete we finish with a
post-treatment that clears the organic growth out of the pores and brightens the surface back
toward new. It costs a little more than a scrape job and lasts several times longer.

We do **not** install zinc or copper strips. Our longevity comes from a thorough kill, keeping
debris off the surface, and a light maintenance visit on a schedule — not a metal strip that
streaks the roof and does little once it's dulled.

## How long do results last?

- **Roofs:** roughly 2–3 years before you'd want another treatment. Shaded, tree-covered roofs are
  on the shorter end.
- **Concrete and pavers:** 1–2 years, longer with the post-treatment and an annual rinse.
- **Fences and walls:** 1–2 years.

The single biggest factor is debris. A roof or walkway that stays covered in fir needles and leaf
litter holds moisture and re-grows moss fast. Keeping it swept and clear, and trimming back
branches that overhang, is what stretches a treatment from two years to four. A recurring
[maintenance plan](/services/maintenance-plans) handles that for you.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is moss actually hurting my roof, or is it just ugly?**
Both. Moss holds water against the shingles and lifts their edges, which shortens roof life. We
cover this in detail in [does moss actually damage your roof](/blog/does-moss-damage-your-roof).

**Can I just wait for a dry summer to kill it?**
No. Moss goes dormant and brown in August, then greens right back up with the first September
rain. Dry weather hides it; it doesn't remove it. See
[can rain wash moss off your roof](/blog/can-rain-wash-moss-off-roof).

**Do you guarantee it won't come back?**
We guarantee the treatment does its job — the moss is killed and removed. We can't guarantee our
climate stops being our climate. That's why maintenance pricing exists.

**Is the treatment safe around kids, pets and plants?**
Yes, handled correctly — we pre-wet and rinse landscaping and control runoff per
<a href="https://ecology.wa.gov/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Department of Ecology</a> guidance. Keep everyone off the treated area until
it's dry.

**What time of year is best?**
Late spring or early fall. We avoid hard freezes.
