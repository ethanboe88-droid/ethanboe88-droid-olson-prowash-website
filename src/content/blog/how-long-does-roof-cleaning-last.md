A properly done [roof soft wash](/services/roof-cleaning) keeps a Pacific Northwest roof visibly
clean and moss-free for **about 2 to 3 years**. Some roofs go longer; heavily shaded ones under
trees are closer to 18 months. The number depends less on the cleaning and more on what happens to
the roof afterward.

## Why "2–3 years" and not "forever"

Moss and algae aren't a one-time infection you cure. They're spores riding in on the wind and the
rain, constantly, landing on a surface that in our climate stays damp for months at a time.
Washington State University's <a href="https://hortsense.cahnr.wsu.edu/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Hortsense</a> describes moss as a
symptom of shade and moisture — remove it today and the same conditions start growing it again
tomorrow.

A good treatment kills what's there down to the root and clears it. That resets the clock. How
fast the clock runs back down is about exposure and debris.

## What shortens your results

- **Shade.** North-facing slopes and tree-covered roofs re-grow two to three times faster than
  sunny exposures.
- **Tree debris.** Fir needles and leaf litter hold moisture on the shingles and feed regrowth.
  This is the #1 accelerant.
- **Clogged gutters.** A backed-up [gutter](/services/gutter-cleaning) keeps the whole roof edge
  wet.
- **Low-slope sections.** Porch roofs and valleys that don't drain fast stay wet and grow moss
  first.
- **A scrape-and-blow "cleaning."** If the last company knocked the moss off dry instead of
  killing it, it regrows in months — and took granules with it on the way out.
- **Pressure washing.** Strips the granules that give algae a rougher surface to grip, and ages
  the shingle so it holds moisture. Never do it. It's against
  <a href="https://www.asphaltroofing.org/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">ARMA</a> guidance and voids most warranties.

## What extends your results

**Keep the roof clear of debris.** Blow or brush the needles and leaves off a couple of times a
year, especially out of the valleys and off the north slopes. This alone can turn a 2-year result
into a 4-year one.

**Trim overhanging branches.** More sun and airflow on the roof, less shade and constant drip. It
also cuts down the debris load.

**Keep the gutters flowing.** Clean them spring and fall so the roof edge can dry between rains.

**Get on a maintenance schedule.** A light re-treatment at the 2-year mark — before moss re-
establishes — is faster and cheaper than waiting for thick growth. Our
[maintenance plans](/services/maintenance-plans) fold roof, gutters and a house wash into one
recurring visit at a lower per-visit rate.

**Skip the zinc strips.** We don't install them and don't recommend them — they streak the roof,
lose effectiveness as they dull, and don't replace keeping the roof clear. A thorough kill plus
debris control does more.

## A simple maintenance rhythm

| Timing | Task |
| --- | --- |
| Twice a year (spring & fall) | Blow debris off the roof; clean the gutters |
| Once a year | Quick visual check — valleys, north slopes, gutter growth |
| Every 2–3 years | Full roof soft wash / re-treatment |
| As needed | Trim branches back off the roofline |

## Frequently asked questions

**Is there a treatment that lasts 10 years?**
No — not honestly, not here. Anyone promising that is selling something. Our climate doesn't allow
it.

**Will one cleaning "cure" my moss problem?**
It clears it and resets the clock. Staying ahead of it after that is maintenance, not another full
restoration.

**How do I know when it's time again?**
Green fuzz returning along the edges and valleys, black streaking coming back, or growth in the
gutters. See [how often should you clean your roof in the PNW](/blog/how-often-clean-roof-pacific-northwest).

**Does a metal roof stay clean longer?**
Usually yes — smoother surface, better drainage — but shaded metal still grows algae and needs
periodic soft washing.
