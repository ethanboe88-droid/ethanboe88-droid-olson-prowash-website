## The problem

This roof sits under big-leaf maples on the north and east sides, and by mid-winter it was
carrying a full load of wet leaves plus established moss along the shaded courses. Moss cushions
that thick hold water against the shingles and lift the edges — left alone, they shorten a roof's
life by years.

The homeowner had been told by another company that the roof "needed pressure washing." It did
not. High pressure on asphalt shingles strips the protective granules and voids the manufacturer
warranty.

## What we did

- **Cleared and protected first.** Loose leaf litter was removed by hand and off the roof, and the
  landscaping, gutters and downspout outlets below were pre-wet and tarped.
- **Low-pressure soft wash.** A professional biocide was fan-sprayed across the whole roof at
  garden-hose pressure to kill the moss, lichen and black algae at the root — no mechanical force
  anywhere near the shingles.
- **Dwell and gentle rinse.** The solution was left to work, then a soft rinse cleared the
  loosened growth.
- **Rinse and gutter flush.** The whole roof was rinsed clean without disturbing the granules,
  and the gutters were flushed clear of everything that came down.

## The result

Clean shingles, an even tone across the whole plane, and no granule loss. The heaviest lichen
spots kept browning and dropping over the following few weeks, which is normal. This roof should
go two to four years before it needs another wash.
