## The problem

Valley humidity and a stand of firs on the uphill side had left this roof with a thick line of
orange needle debris down the main valley and moss creeping across the shaded courses. The valley
is the one spot on a roof you never want holding debris — it's the drainage path for two whole
planes.

## What we did

- **Cleared the valley by hand,** then off the roof, so the drainage path was open before any
  water went on it.
- **Low-pressure soft wash** across the shaded slopes with a professional biocide to kill the
  moss and algae at the root — no pressure on the shingles.
- **Dwell, rinse, and a zinc treatment** along the ridge to slow how fast it comes back at this
  elevation.
- **Gutters flushed** of everything that came down during the wash.

## The result

The valley is clear and the whole roof reads one tone again. In the Skykomish valley, roofs like
this one do best on a two-to-three-year wash cycle — a [maintenance plan](/services/maintenance-plans)
keeps each visit quick and cheaper because the growth never gets a full head start.
